JOURNEY 01 / BalanceTest

Your fatty-acid picture

What it measures

11 fatty acids in a dried blood spot, with calculated ratios and indices.

A fatty-acid profile is not a diagnosis and does not establish a health benefit from supplementation.

  1. Frame the questionDecide what you want to learn about dietary fatty-acid exposure. You do not have to buy a test to start learning.
  2. Understand the methodRead the manufacturer's current instructions. A dried-blood-spot profile and a red-blood-cell Omega-3 Index are not automatically interchangeable.
  3. Read in contextKeep the sample date, method and units with the result. Take questions about interpretation to a qualified clinician.
  4. Choose a follow-upDiscuss food, habits and whether another measurement would change a decision. A before-and-after result does not prove which change caused it.

JOURNEY 02 / Gut Health Test

Gut markers, with context

What it measures

Tryptophan, kynurenine and indole-3-propionic acid in a dried blood spot, plus calculated ratios and a manufacturer-defined index.

This is not a stool microbiome inventory, an allergy test, or a diagnosis of a digestive condition. An association between a marker and health is not proof that changing it improves health.

  1. Separate the questionsDistinguish curiosity about a biomarker from a symptom needing clinical assessment. Do not postpone care while waiting for a home test.
  2. Know what is sampledThe manufacturer describes a blood-spot test, not stool sequencing. Follow the supplied collection and shipping instructions.
  3. Keep uncertainty visibleA composite score does not map every organism or explain every symptom. Ask what evidence supports an action based on this particular report.
  4. Review before repeatingKeep any repeat measurement comparable. Discuss whether repeat testing is useful, rather than assuming that every change requires another kit.

JOURNEY 03 / Vitamin D Test

Vitamin D, without guesswork

What it measures

25-hydroxyvitamin D, a blood marker used to assess vitamin D status.

Routine vitamin D screening is not recommended for every healthy adult. A result is not an automatic instruction to start or increase a supplement.

  1. Ask whether testing helpsThe 2024 Endocrine Society guideline advises against routine screening in generally healthy adults without an established indication. Ask whether a result would change your care.
  2. Check the detailsRead the current collection instructions and keep the report's units. Nanograms per milliliter and nanomoles per liter are different units.
  3. Discuss the whole pictureBring your medicine and supplement list to the clinician interpreting the result. This website does not calculate a dose.
  4. Agree on a planFollow-up timing depends on the clinical reason for testing. A manufacturer's retest interval is not a universal medical recommendation.

JOURNEY 04 / HbA1c Test

A longer view of blood sugar

What it measures

Glycated hemoglobin, which reflects average blood glucose over roughly the previous three months.

A home wellness result is not a standalone diagnosis. It does not show real-time glucose or every high and low, and several conditions can affect its interpretation.

  1. Start with your clinicianIf you have symptoms, an existing diagnosis or questions about screening, use clinical care rather than a website pathway to choose a test.
  2. Understand the timeframeHbA1c is a longer-term marker. It cannot tell you your glucose level right now or show the effect of yesterday's meal.
  3. Check interpretation limitsAnemia, hemoglobin variants and changes in red-cell lifespan can affect results. Tell your clinician about relevant conditions and medicines.
  4. Follow up appropriatelyUnexpected results need professional interpretation and sometimes confirmatory testing. Do not change prescribed medicines based on this page or a home report.
