# K2 draft — updated evidence screen

NON-PUBLIC. Prepared 2026-09-06 by the coding assistant for a qualified reviewer; no independent clinical review is claimed. Supplements, full papers and a complete search remain outstanding. Read alongside the [existing K2 draft](../editorial-hero-review/VITAMIN_K2_DRAFT.md), which must not be published in isolation from this update.

Search scope: web-indexed PubMed results for vitamin K2 randomized trials and calcification, emphasizing 2025–2026; abstracts and bibliographic records for the following primary trials were inspected. This is a bounded screen, not a systematic review.

| Primary record | What the abstract reports | Editorial boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Vossen et al., JAMA Cardiology 2026, PMID 42268593](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42268593/) | VitaK-CAC randomized 180 patients; the abstract describes 85 receiving MK-7 and 82 placebo. Over two years, CAC progression differed between groups (P=.02) in symptomatic coronary disease. | Imaging-marker result in a selected population, not established prevention of clinical events or proof for a marketed D3/K2 mixture. | [Hasific et al., JACC Advances 2023, PMID 38938724](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38938724/) | A secondary coronary analysis from the K2+D trial reported no statistically significant overall CAC progression difference (P=.089); a higher-baseline-CAC subgroup differed (P=.047). | Do not present a subgroup as the overall finding, or count this related analysis as an entirely independent trial from the earlier aortic-valve publication. |
| [Haroon et al., Kidney International Reports 2023, PMID 37705910](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37705910/) | In hemodialysis patients, 178 were randomized and 138 completed follow-up. The 18-month coronary-calcification result did not differ significantly; reported relative mean difference 0.85, 95% CI 0.55–1.31. | Selected clinical population and attrition limit transfer to a general wellness audience. Absence of a significant difference is not proof of equivalence or universal ineffectiveness. |

## Proposed reader-draft addition — still unapproved

Studies do not all test the same population, formulation or outcome. A newer trial reported a difference in an imaging measure of coronary calcification, while earlier studies had different or inconclusive results. An imaging change is not the same as evidence that a supplement prevents a heart attack. Look at the full study and its limitations before applying a headline to a product or to yourself. [2026 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42268593/), [2023 coronary analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38938724/)
