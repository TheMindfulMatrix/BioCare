# Vitamin K2: understand the form before the promise

NON-PUBLIC REFINED DRAFT — September 10, 2026. Editorial revision completed; clinical, company/compliance and publication approval pending. Read with the [editorial review and source ledger](K2_EDITORIAL_REVIEW.md). The September 6 manuscript is preserved separately.

## What the name tells you

Vitamin K is a family: K1 is phylloquinone, while K2 includes menaquinones such as MK-4 and MK-7. Vitamin K supports clotting and bone-related protein processes. Leafy greens supply K1; some animal and fermented foods supply menaquinones. A nutrient's biological role does not tell us whether adding a particular supplement improves a person's health. [NIH ODS Vitamin K](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/)

The useful study questions are specific: which form, which participants, which comparison and which outcome? A change in a blood marker, a bone scan, or a coronary scan answers a different question from whether people experience fewer fractures or heart attacks.

## What have trials measured?

Bone findings vary. A three-year study in healthy postmenopausal women reported less bone loss at some measured sites with MK-7, but not at the total hip. A later three-year trial in women with osteopenia found improvement in an osteocalcin marker without a corresponding difference in bone density or microarchitecture; both groups also received calcium and vitamin D. These differences matter when interpreting a promise about stronger bones. Neither finding establishes that every K2 product prevents fractures. [2013 primary trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23525894/), [2021 primary trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33030563/)

Cardiovascular studies also ask different questions. In a 2022 trial of men with existing aortic valve calcification, MK-7 plus vitamin D did not significantly reduce the progression of that calcification over two years. A 2026 trial in symptomatic coronary disease reported slower progression of a coronary-calcium imaging measure with MK-7; the measure increased in both groups. The researchers left the meaning for plaque stability uncertain. An imaging difference does not establish that a supplement prevents a heart attack. [2022 primary trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35465686/), [2026 primary trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42268593/)

## What to look for on a label

Write down the named K form, amount per serving and number of capsules in that serving. If D3 appears too, record it separately. A combination label does not by itself demonstrate an added benefit, and a trial of one form or mixture cannot automatically validate another. If the label omits a detail, ask for the current label instead of filling the gap from another brand.

General vitamin-K intake guidance does not establish a separate K2 supplement target. The absence of an established vitamin-K upper limit is also not a reason to assume unlimited intake is appropriate. [NIH intake and safety guidance](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/)

## Medication context comes first

Vitamin K can interact seriously with warfarin and similar vitamin-K-antagonist anticoagulants. Consistent intake from food and supplements matters. Before starting, stopping or changing a vitamin-K supplement, speak with your prescriber or pharmacist; do not change your medicine yourself. Bring your full medication and supplement list. [NIH medication guidance](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/)

Ask: does this study test the same ingredient and the outcome I care about, in people like me? Keep that question separate from whether a particular supplement belongs in your care. This is general education, not a diagnosis, treatment plan or recommendation to buy a product.
